Stability of parton distributions at high x : Impact of nuclear and power corrections

We present a comprehensive new global QCD analysis of unpolarized parton distribution functions (PDFs) based upon proton, deuteron, and A = 3 data, including the latest inclusive deep-inelastic scattering (DIS) measurements from Jefferson Lab at high Bjorken- x . Using the JAM Bayesian Monte Carlo framework, we systematically explore the stability of the PDFs with respect to variations in the cuts on the invariant mass W of the DIS final state, the implementation of target mass and higher twist corrections, as well as on the nuclear wave functions for the A = 2 and 3 data. Isospin effects and symmetry energy in nuclear reactions

Isospin and the symmetry energy are linked within the symmetry term of the liquid-drop mass formula and within the corresponding term of the equation of state of asymmetric nuclear matter. The Pauli exclusion principle leads to a leading quadratic dependence of the kinetic part of the symmetry term of the nuclear binding-energy formula on the neutron-proton asymmetry (N-Z)/A. Charge symmetry ensures the same leading quadratic dependence for the potential part. Charge invariance further generalizes this dependence, to lowest order, from T$_3^2$ to T(T+1). The strength of the symmetry term, in contrast, is determined by the symmetry energy. At sub-saturation densities the symmetry energy can be constrained by nuclear-structure observables, such as neutron skin thicknesses, isovector collective modes and the related electric dipole polarizability, and excitation energies of isobaric analog states. It can also be constrained by reaction observables, such as charge exchange and elastic scattering cross sections, isoscaling and isospin diffusion. At supra-saturation densities, by contrast, the only means of studying asymmetric nuclear matter under laboratory conditions are relativistic heavy-ion collisions. These studies use differential observables such as neutron/proton flows and light isobar or $\pi^{-}/\pi^{+}$ ratios. Complementary information can be obtained from neutron star observations including gravitational waves. The latest measurements and analyses of the stiffness of the symmetry energy will be presented, together with the recent NA61/SHINE observation of an unexpectedly large isospin-symmetry violation in kaon production. Attention will also be paid to the challenges of measuring charged and neutral products of relativistic heavy-ion collisions in the context of the recent ASY-EOS II experiment at GSI and to the new detectors used in the setup.

Perspective on Proton Transversity Distribution Functions

Using available information on proton tensor charges and constraints from perturbative quantum chromodynamics (QCD), Ansaetze are introduced for hadron-scale in-proton valence-quark transversity distributions functions (DFs). The location of the upper edge of the pair-instability supernovae black hole mass gap

Abstract Gravitational wave observations are beginning to probe the upper edge of the pair-instability supernova (PISN) black hole mass gap, a key prediction of stellar evolution. In this work, we quantify the sensitivity of this boundary to stellar-evolution inputs and modeling choices using a suite of simulations that vary inputs including nuclear reaction rates, mixing processes, and stellar winds. We find that variations of the 12C(α, γ)16O reaction rate produce the largest shift in the upper edge, ΔM ∼ 34 M⊙, with the triple-α rate producing a comparable shift of ∼24 M⊙. Notably, 16O + 16O reactions shift the upper edge by ∼15 M⊙ while leaving the lower edge unchanged, implying they can widen or narrow the mass gap. Other processes affect the location at the ≲ 10 M⊙ level. In contrast to the lower edge, we find that the upper edge is robust to variations in spatial and temporal resolution, indicating that it is reliably resolved in current simulations. Our results demonstrate that the upper edge is most sensitive to nuclear reaction rates and, while comparatively less affected by astrophysical contamination than the lower edge, provides a direct probe of the nuclear processes governing pair instability. We discuss the implications for interpreting high-mass black hole detections in gravitational wave data.

The Price of Discovery: Cost-Optimized Detectors for the GUT-Scale QCD Axion

Grand unification and string theory point to a quantum chromodynamics (QCD) axion with mass between $10^{-11}$ and $10^{-8}$ eV, while heterotic string theory suggests a mass of $0.5-0.8$ neV. We study which laboratory experiment could discover or exclude DFSZ-strength QCD axion dark matter at and above 0.5 neV at the lowest cost, which we estimate from scaling laws calibrated to historical data. We find that a lumped-element detector, building on the ABRACADABRA concept, can do so with demonstrated readout technology; an example is a shielded toroid about 14 m across with a 6 T NbTi magnet, whose magnet and cryostat we estimate at around 120 million Swiss francs (MCHF). If projected readout targets are met, a 5 m toroid at 25 MCHF suffices. We find these designs by optimizing the toroid, solenoid, and core lumped-element topologies under a common cost metric. The toroid emerges as the most promising and cost-effective topology. The optimal field of the toroid detector is far below the 16 T of the proposed DMRadio-GUT design, which would cost nearly twice as much as our optimum. Heterodyne searches with superconducting radio-frequency cavities, with cavity shapes that we optimize for price, cost between about the same as and twice as much as the lumped-element detectors, but require cavities of volumes $\sim50-2000$ m$^3$. We find that nuclear-magnetic-resonance searches (CASPEr) need orders-of-magnitude gains in spin polarization and coherence. We release axionMultiPhysics, an open-source finite-element and multiphysics package for, e.g., magnetoquasistatic and full-wave electromagnetics, superconductors, readout circuits, and mechanics, from which most of our numerical results were obtained.

Density functional theory of renormalization group in nuclear matter

We present the density functional renormalization group (density-fRG), in which density fluctuations are separated from the quantum fluctuations in the vacuum and are integrated out successively within the functional renormalization group approach. This allows us to quantify medium effects and to study the physics of high baryon densities. The method is applied to the nucleon-meson effective field theory, also known as the Walecka model, where the density dependence of the nucleon-meson Yukawa couplings is generated self-consistently by their flow equations. It is found that both the attractive and the repulsive nucleon-meson interactions are screened by the dense medium. This results in an equation of state of symmetric nuclear matter that is stiffer than in the relativistic mean-field approximation for $\rho_0\lesssim\rho\lesssim 2.5\,\rho_0$, and becomes softer for $\rho\gtrsim 2.5\,\rho_0$, where $\rho_0$ is the saturation density. Furthermore, a new phenomenon called the locking of the Fermi surface is found: the effective energy of the quasi-nucleon stays close to the Fermi surface, while both are running with the renormalization group scale.

Detailed experimental study of the 59 Cu( p , α ) 56 Ni reaction and constraints on its astrophysical reaction rate

The 59 Cu ( p , α ) 56 Ni reaction plays an important role in explosive astrophysical scenarios such as Type I X-ray bursts and the ν p -process in neutrino-driven winds following a core-collapse supernova, where it regulates the flow of nucleosynthesis through the NiCu cycle and the synthesis of heavier nuclei. We present a direct measurement of the 59 C u ( p , α ) 56 N i excitation function from 2.43–5.88~MeV in the center-of-mass frame, performed in inverse kinematics with the high-efficiency MUSIC active-target detector at FRIB. The angle- and energy-integrated cross sections extend direct measurements to lower energies than previously reported and remove the angular-integration model dependence of earlier work. To extrapolate the rate to astrophysical energies, we constrain the statistical-model description through a systematic optimization of the DEM-3 α -optical model potential geometry, and quantify the model-selection uncertainty with a Bayesian model averaging analysis over 96 TALYS combinations. The resulting stellar rate carries a temperature-dependent uncertainty factor of 1.26–1.63 over T 9 = 0.2 –10 and is systematically lower than the REACLIB evaluation, remaining below the competing ( p , γ ) rate for T 9 ≲ 3.94 . These results substantially weaken the inferred NiCu cycle strength and establish the 59 Cu ( p , γ ) 60 Zn rate as the dominant remaining uncertainty.
