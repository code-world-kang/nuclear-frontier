Thermal effects on stellar neutron capture reactions: a quantum dynamical approach

Abstract The neutron capture process plays a vital role in creating the heavy elements in the universe. Astrophysical environments involved in these processes are characterized by two distinct reaction mechanisms: the slow and rapid neutron capture processes. In this work, the slow neutron capture process is described with the time-dependent coupled channels wave-packet (TDCCWP) method that uses both a many-body nuclear potential and an initial temperature-dependent state to account for the thermal environment. To evaluate the role of a mixed and entangled initial state in the temperature-dependent neutron capture cross section, TDCCWP calculations are compared with those from the coupled-channels density matrix (CCDM) method based on the Lindblad master equation. The importance of including temperature in the initial wave-function of the TDCCWP approach is compared to a thermalisation of the reaction rate using a Hauser–Feshbach style approach. TDCCWP calculations indicate a decrease of the n+ $$^{188}$$ 188 Os capture cross section with increasing temperature, along with a decrease in reaction rates for the highest thermal energies studied, which are contrary to Hauser–Feshbach calculations and important in the rapid neutron capture process. The physical reason for this discrepancy is the key role of the dynamical nuclear coupling between the thermally populated states of the target nucleus, which is neglected in the Hauser–Feshbach approach, but creates a dominant neutron capture pathway with increased neutron speed and thus reduces the neutron capture cross section.

Structure of multi-$$\Lambda $$ hypernuclei with a Skyrme-type $$\Lambda \Lambda $$ interaction guided by double-$$\Lambda $$ hypernuclear and neutron-star constraints

We investigate multi- $$\Lambda $$ hypernuclei using a Skyrme-type $$\Lambda \Lambda $$ energy density functional whose s-wave parameters are fitted to double- $$\Lambda $$ hypernuclear information supplemented by pseudodata for heavier systems. The remaining parameters are explored over ranges guided by the double- $$\Lambda $$ fit quality and neutron-star observations. We examine homogeneous nucleon– $$\Lambda $$ matter near normal nuclear density and perform spherical Hartree–Fock calculations without pairing for finite systems built on stable doubly magic cores from oxygen to lead. Within the explored parameter ranges, the $$\Lambda $$ chemical potential and the corresponding emission threshold in homogeneous matter show a larger variation with the repulsive p-wave coupling than the net variation obtained by changing the density-dependent coupling and refitting the s-wave parameters. In finite nuclei, the $$\Lambda $$ radius, separation energy, and single-particle structure become increasingly sensitive to the p-wave term as the number of $$\Lambda $$ hyperons grows, whereas the nucleonic matter radius changes only weakly. These results quantify the finite-nucleus manifestations of p-wave $$\Lambda \Lambda $$ interactions over a neutron-star-guided parameter domain, linking astrophysical constraints to hypernuclear structure near normal density.

Energies and rates of α-decay of unknown nuclei in the region Z ≥ 100

We consider the applicability of phenomenological methods for determining the characteristics of α-decay in transfermium region. Similar to the local mass relations method used to predict the masses of unknown nuclides, we use local relations based on the Q α values of neighboring nuclei to estimate α-decay energies. Based on the AME2020 compilation and modern experimental data, estimates of Q α were obtained for nuclei in the range 100 ≥ Z ≥ 118. To estimate the α-decay half-life, we examined various phenomenological dependencies and adjusted the parameters taking into account modern experimental data. At this stage, the best description of the experiment is achieved using the Viola-Seaborg formula with different sets of parameters for even-even, odd, and odd-odd nuclei. A comparison was made with the predictions of other models.

Decay Modes of the Newly Synthesised 231 Am Isotope

Decay systematics of the newly synthesised 231 Am isotope were studied within the framework of the Coulomb and Proximity Potential Model (CPPM), and the results are compared with the predictions of the Universal Decay Law (UDL), the Modified Universal Decay Law (MUDL), and the Horoi formula. Principles of locus-specific chromatin remodeling induced by therapeutic DNA integration

Understanding how integrated genetic elements engage host chromatin is essential for improving gene-therapy safety. Lentiviral vectors (LVs) are widely used, yet how integrated cis-regulatory elements (CREs) interact with the three-dimensional (3D) host genome remains poorly understood. Partitioning biological and physical sources of N 2 supersaturation in inland waters using natural 15 N 15 N signatures

Inland waters are hotspots of biogenic dinitrogen (N 2 ) emission, serving as a permanent sink for anthropogenic reactive nitrogen (N). Although N 2 emission is commonly inferred from N 2 supersaturation, the extent to which this supersaturation reflects biological contribution remains poorly understood. Here, we combine dissolved N 2 /Argon ratio (N 2 /Ar) with N 2 clumped isotope composition to quantify the biogenic fraction of supersaturated N 2 in two deep lakes with contrasting redox conditions. Our results show that biogenic N 2 accounts for only 59 ± 30% of the observed N 2 supersaturation, with the remainder coming from physical bubble injection, which elevates N 2 /Ar ratios without contributing to N removal. Bubble-injected N 2 is especially pronounced in inflowing rivers, indicating that riverine bubble input can propagate into lake interiors and bias whole-lake N 2 budgets. These findings demonstrate that N 2 supersaturation is not a reliable proxy for biological N 2 production and imply that previous N 2 saturation–based studies may largely have overestimated N removal capacities of inland aquatic ecosystems.

Observable signatures of a quarkyonic phase in neutron stars

Quarkyonic matter in \(\beta\)-equilibrium is a potential description of cold dense matter in neutron stars (NSs), that introduces non-interacting quarks alongside nucleons and leptons in NS cores. In this paper, we impose observational and theoretical constraints on the model to perform Bayesian inference on it, and find that it is possible to have quarkyonic matter equations of state that satisfy all current astrophysical observations, thereby reinforcing the argument for its use alongside traditional ones. To differentiate between NSs where a quarkyonic phase does and does not appear in the core, we identify some novel signatures based on the mass-radius relation. Focusing on canonical (\(1.4\ \mathrm{M_\odot}\)) NSs, we find the populations of NSs with and without quarkyonic cores show separability on the basis of the slope and curvatures of the mass-radius curve, the central sound speed of the star, and the radius difference between two NSs of \(2\ \mathrm{M_\odot}\) and \(1.4\ \mathrm{M_\odot}\). Our results indicate that observing a neutron star with these signatures matching the values for quarkyonic core NSs would provide a strong evidence for the existence of a quarkyonic phase or a similar crossover transition in its core.

Lipidated macrocyclic peptide S-880008 inhibits SARS-CoV-2 fusion by suppressing protomer dissociation at conserved spike sites

Viral infections remain a persistent global threat, necessitating development of broad-spectrum antiviral agents targeting conserved sites to combat emerging variants. Here, we develop S-880008, a lipid-modified macrocyclic peptide identified through mRNA display screening and structural optimization, as a novel class of fusion inhibitor of SARS-CoV-2. S-880008 exhibits potent broad-spectrum antiviral activity against diverse variants including Omicrons. Cryo-electron microscopy reveals that S-880008 employs an unprecedented dual-binding mechanism targeting conserved, cryptic sites: the macrocyclic peptide portion anchors the receptor-binding domain (RBD)–subdomain 1 interface, while the lipid chain penetrates the N-terminal domain of the adjacent protomer. These interactions enforce an RBD 3-up conformation, suppressing protomer dissociation and blocking viral fusion. Crucially, intranasal administration of S-880008 significantly reduces viral titers and improves survival in a murine infection model. These findings highlight a novel mechanism whereby S-880008 deeply engages conserved interprotomer regions, providing a rationale for designing broad-spectrum antiviral fusion inhibitors. S-880008 is a lipidated macrocyclic peptide that broadly inhibits SARS-CoV-2 variants. By locking spike proteins in a fusion-incompetent state through interactions with conserved sites, it blocks viral entry and protects against infection in vivo.

Start-to-end commissioning simulation for the Hefei advanced light facility storage ring

The Hefei Advanced Light Facility (HALF) is a fourth-generation diffraction-limited synchrotron light source for which construction began in 2023. The storage ring has an extremely low emittance of less than 100 pm $$\cdot$$ rad with an energy of 2.2 GeV. It contains 20 super-periods of the modified hybrid six-bend achromat with a circumference of approximately 480 m. A real storage ring is different from the ideal model due to various sources of error, such as the misalignment of components, imperfect magnetic fields, and RF cavity errors. Owing to the strong nonlinear effects and small dynamic apertures, these errors have a serious effect on the low-emittance storage ring, which presents major difficulties in its commissioning. To determine the practical performance of the HALF storage ring with these errors and verify the effectiveness of its commissioning, a start-to-end commissioning simulation was performed. In addition, the commissioning simulation process provides the basis for the development of the commissioning software. Details and results of the commissioning simulation are reported.

Mitochondrial fission factor senses and governs ferroptosis

Ferroptosis is a form of regulated cell death driven by iron-dependent lipid peroxidation and is implicated in diverse pathological states1–4. Although mitochondria and other organelles are increasingly being recognized as important modulators of ferroptosis5–7, a unifying mechanism that couples organelle dynamics to ferroptotic execution has remained elusive. Here using quantitative phosphoproteomics, we identified mitochondrial fission factor (MFF) as a key ferroptosis-selective regulator. Mechanistically, the lipid mediator 17-HETE promotes phosphorylation of MFF at Ser155, which triggers the coordinated fragmentation and dysfunction of mitochondria and peroxisomes. This remodelling intensifies interorganelle crosstalk, amplifies oxidative stress and accelerates ferroptotic death. To monitor this phosphorylation event in living cells, we developed MFF–SPARK, a phase-separation-based biosensor, for real-time tracking of ferroptosis through MFF activation. Using MFF–SPARK, we identified PKCβ and DUSP22 as a coordinated kinase–phosphatase pair that governs MFF phosphorylation. We also discovered avermectin B1 as a pharmacological activator of the PKCβ–MFF axis, which can sensitize tumours to ferroptosis in vivo. Together, our findings establish MFF phosphorylation as a central regulatory node in ferroptosis-associated organelle remodelling and provide a conceptual framework and toolbox for monitoring and pharmacologically interrogating ferroptosis. The mitochondrial–peroxisomal fission adaptor MFF is identified as a selective pro-ferroptotic mediator, and the newly developed MFF–SPARK sensor enables high-content screening of ferroptosis-sensitizing compounds and the discovery of upstream regulators of MFF.

Differential active adhesion and a capillary instability drive global eversion and dissemination of bacterial colonies

Attractive forces between cells can determine the shape and sorting behaviour of bacterial colonies. During colony development, chemical gradients form within the colony, but it is unclear how they affect cohesion. Here we discover that such gradients trigger global eversion of proliferating colonies formed by the human pathogen Neisseria gonorrhoeae. Like a jet, the inner core flows towards the periphery where it is partially dispersed and partially spreads around the core of the colony. Living dispersed cells reform colonies, leading to rapid dissemination. The eversion depends on local oxygen depletion, which reduces the activity of the molecular motors that govern self-attraction: before the eversion, the colony consists of a weakly cohesive spherical core surrounded by a strongly cohesive shell. An idealized computational model reveals that, in this configuration, an instability analogous to a capillary instability emerges when the thickness of the strongly interacting shell falls below a critical value. We show that the probability for eversion strongly depends on the motor-generated attractive force between bacteria. Overall, our results indicate that chemical gradients induce spontaneous symmetry breaking in a mechanically active colony, thereby triggering a nonlinear shape instability. This instability generates large-scale cellular fluxes that redistribute bacteria towards environments more favourable for their growth. Chemical gradients help shape bacterial colonies. Now it is shown that these gradients can lead to a nonlinear shape instability in a colony of Neisseria gonorrhoeae.
