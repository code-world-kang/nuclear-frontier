Testable leptogenesis and 0 ν β β decay in extended seesaw model

We investigate the possibility of observable neutrinoless double beta decay ( 0 ν β β ) and viable leptogenesis within a low-scale extended inverse seesaw mechanism with additional sterile neutrinos. General effective field theory considerations suggest that if there are experimentally observable signatures in 0 ν β β decay and a lepton asymmetry generated by heavy right-handed neutrino decays, thermal leptogenesis is likely to be unviable. However, in this work, we show that, in the context of low-scale leptogenesis, one can obtain the observed baryon asymmetry of the Universe and observable signatures of 0 ν β β decay in the presence of additional sterile neutrinos. In this framework, the light neutrino masses are suppressed by the extended seesaw parameter μ , thereby allowing for O ( 10 TeV ) right-handed neutrinos while avoiding small Yukawa couplings as in other neutrino mass models and near degeneracies in the right-handed neutrino spectrum as required by the low-scale leptogenesis paradigm. Contributions to the 0 ν β β -decay rate from additional sterile neutrinos can be appreciable, while the corresponding contributions to the early Universe lepton asymmetry washout rate are suppressed by other parameters not entering the 0 ν β β -decay amplitudes. We show that, for keV–MeV scale, sterile neutrinos future ton-scale 0 ν β β -decay experiments offer potential signals while maintaining viable leptogenesis.

Light-particle emission in nucleon-induced fission of $^{235,238}$U at energies from 10 MeV to 1 GeV

Background: In nucleon-induced fission up to GeV energies, early nonequilibrium emission alters the residual nuclei before statistical evaporation and scission. The emitted light particles therefore probe each reaction stage while supplying needed nuclear data. Purpose: We study neutron and light charged-particle emission in $^{235,238}$U$(n,f)$ and $^{235,238}$U$(p,f)$ reactions at incident energies from 10 MeV to 1 GeV. Methods: The reactions are simulated event by event by coupling the isospin-dependent quantum molecular dynamics model with the GEMINI++ statistical decay code. Results: The calculations follow the measured rise in average neutron multiplicity with incident energy, while yielding higher values above 100 MeV and in the proton-induced prescission component. Within the coupled calculations, the neutron multiplicity growth comes mainly from statistical evaporation before scission, whereas emission from the fission fragments changes weakly. Although the average preequilibrium neutron yield remains small, early emission spreads the residual nuclei across multiple elements and wide excitation energies. At high energies, protons are emitted predominantly before equilibration, directly reducing the charge retained by the residual nuclei. Conclusions: In the coupled calculations, statistical evaporation before scission governs the neutron multiplicity growth, whereas early nonequilibrium emission shapes the residual nuclei and dominates high-energy proton emission. These results provide a reference for modeling intermediate-energy fission, in which prescission emission changes the charge, mass, and excitation energy of the fissioning system.

Systematic Parameter Optimization of Quantum Molecular Dynamics Models Using Multi-Ion Fragmentation Data: Implications for Hadron Therapy

Quantum molecular dynamics (QMD) models are widely used to simulate nuclear fragmentation in hadron therapy, but their predictive accuracy depends strongly on parameters that are often selected empirically. We developed an optimized QMD framework by systematically calibrating three parameters for a relativistic mean-field model with the NS2 parameter set and Skyrme models with the SLy4 and SkM* parameter sets: the wave-packet width L, maximum evolution time Tm, and impact-parameter envelope factor benv. The wave-packet width was determined from experimental charge radii, whereas Tm and benv were parameterized as functions of incident kinetic energy and reaction-system mass and optimized using proton- and heavy-ion-induced fragmentation data over 30-400 MeV/u. Performance was compared with the original LiQMD, Binary Cascade, and Liege Intranuclear Cascade models. The optimized Tm depended strongly on incident energy but only weakly on system mass, indicating that the transition from the dynamical QMD stage to statistical de-excitation is governed mainly by collision energy. In contrast, benv showed model-dependent behavior: NS2 favored larger peripheral-collision contributions for lighter systems at low energies, whereas the Skyrme models showed relatively weak energy and mass dependence. The optimized parameterizations improved agreement with experimental fragment production cross sections, angular distributions, and energy distributions. The optimized Skyrme models achieved the best overall performance and outperformed the cascade models for most datasets. This framework provides a physically consistent description of nuclear fragmentation across multiple observables and may improve calculations of secondary-particle transport, dose deposition, and linear energy transfer in hadron therapy.

Subsolar neutron star-black hole binaries from collapsars: Gravitational waveform models and detectability in current searches

Detecting subsolar objects in binary mergers would be an exceptional discovery in gravitational wave astronomy. So far, dedicated searches for subsolar coalescences reported no confident detections, setting upper bounds on their merger rate. Given that standard stellar evolution is not able to produce subsolar candidates, a confident detection would point either to new physics or to unconventional astrophysical pathways. These include collapsars, where the outer parts of neutrino-cooled accretion disks around newborn stellar-mass black holes are subject to fragmentation and give rise to subsolar neutron stars, which can later merge with the central black hole. We show that current subsolar searches may miss a significant fraction of these neutron star-black hole mergers, because their templates do not account for the small mass ratios, the tidal disruption of the neutron star, and the effects of the surrounding gas. This result motivates improving the waveform modeling used in current subsolar searches and building adequate template banks tailored to these systems.
